PREDICATE CHESS
Forcing Clamp 1 — Woodpecker Exercises 51–100
Policy and 50-puzzle worked trace corpus
Version 0.2

This file contains the Predicate Chess policy and all 50 exercises supplied in the Woodpecker PGN (Exercises 51–100).
The traces are deliberately bounded, human-inspectable proofs under the policy boundary rather than unrestricted engine analyses.
Every source main line was legality-checked against its FEN. Reply classes are policy proofs: they close only the tactical objective declared for that exercise.

======================================================================
PREDICATE-CHESS/1
policy: forcing-clamp-1
======================================================================

[profile]

see_after: 1
try_budget: 10
check_horizon: 2
forcing_reply_probe: 1
quiet_budget: 0
repair_budget: 0
behind_quiet_budget: 0
gain_goal: minor_piece

[profile semantics]

see_after: 1
A candidate may be generated from predicates in the current position and predicates visible in the position immediately after that candidate. It may not be generated from a speculative position two or more plies ahead.

try_budget: 10
Every TRY enters one position and consumes one unit. A reply closed by a named rule is represented but does not consume a TRY.

check_horizon: 2
The solver remembers how many of its own checking moves have been followed in the current branch. After two, it may answer a required check, CASH a visible target, COMPARE visible capture orders, or mate. It may not begin a new tactical theme from newly imagined deeper predicates.

forcing_reply_probe: 1
A closure rule may inspect one forcing opponent move after its witness. It may also inspect the immediate forced answer needed to decide whether the gain survives. The probe may verify an existing theme but may not start a new one.

quiet_budget: 0
A candidate must be forcing in the Predicate Chess sense: check, capture, mate threat, promotion threat, attack on a queen or loose piece, attack or pin of a defender, fork, skewer, or line-opening move. A pin such as Bc4 is permitted; an unrelated improving move is not.

repair_budget: 0
The solver may not return later and invent a preparatory repair move. A curing move must already have been generated from predicates observed at the original position.

behind_quiet_budget: 0
When behind material, every own move must check, threaten mate, win material, or directly attack a named tactical target.

gain_goal: minor_piece
Unless a puzzle declares a stricter objective, a material proof succeeds after a net gain of at least one minor piece survives the forcing-reply clamp.

[thought states]

OBSERVE
List the relevant predicates in the entered position. No candidate may be generated before this happens.

THREAT(goal,theme)
Select and try a forcing move that uses the observed predicates to impose the named goal.

REPLIES(goal)
Represent every legal opponent reply. Mark each reply LIVE or close it with a named rule and witness. Every LIVE reply must be TRYed.

CASH(target)
Convert an already visible loose, forked, skewered, pinned, or otherwise bound target.

COMPARE(options)
Compare visible move orders, normally two captures. Probe each option through the opponent's strongest LIVE reply. Reject an option after its first refutation.

FIX(goal_move,defect,budget)
A future state for curing one explicit defect. It is disabled in this profile because repair_budget is zero.

PROVED, HORIZON, GIVEUP
Terminal states. PROVED is proof within this policy's declared objective and clamp, not a claim of complete engine evaluation.

[notation]

Moves use SAN. Predicate piece references use PIECE@square. In this prototype corpus, the square and current position determine color; a future stricter serializer may write wN@e4 and bB@f6 explicitly.

USES lists named predicates; it is not a numeric evaluation.

[trace language]

THINK <state>.
SAW <relevant current-position predicates>.
CANDIDATES <move: one-ply predicates; USES=[current predicates]>; ...
TRY <move> BECAUSE <named rule or explicit selection fact>.
REPLY <move or exact reply class>: <reply predicates> :: LIVE.
REPLY <move or exact reply class>: <reply predicates> :: CLOSED BY <rule> WITH <witness>.
FROM <move> :: <refutation or failure reason>.
PROVED.

[selection rules]

SEE-FIRST
Do not generate or order candidates before SAW. Every candidate must name at least one observed predicate in USES. Checks and mate threats count as current-position predicates even though their moves are one ply out.

PICK-OUR-MOVE
Choose the first nonempty group below:
1. Mate.
2. Check with exactly one legal reply class.
3. Capture that immediately reaches the objective.
4. Check with the most USES.
5. Other forcing move with the most USES.
Within one group, prefer fewer legal reply classes. If still tied, keep the generator's deterministic move order.

USES
USES is an explicit list, not an engine score. A move USES a predicate when it captures or attacks its loose piece, attacks or pins its defender, changes its alignment, reduces its mobility, creates a fork or skewer from it, or advances its immediate passed-pawn threat.

ALL-REPLIES
After our TRY, every legal opponent move must be represented by REPLY. A reply may be omitted from deeper search only through a named closure rule whose predicates apply in that reply position.

TRY-REPLIES
Try LIVE opponent replies in this order:
1. Check.
2. Capture the attacker or winning piece.
3. Save or continue defending the target.
4. Create mate in one.
5. Create an equal-or-greater material counterthreat.
6. Remaining LIVE replies.

FIRST-REFUTATION-STOPS
One opponent reply that defeats one of our candidates refutes that candidate. The candidate's remaining opponent replies need not then be searched.

REPLY-CLASS
Legal replies may share one REPLY line only when the class is disjoint, the listed predicates apply to every move in it, and every move has the same closure rule and witness shape. The reply classes at a node must cover every legal reply.

[closure rules]

CLOSE-MATE-IN-ONE
Close a reply when the resulting position has a named mate in one.

CLOSE-ABANDON
Close a reply when it abandons a bound target, a named immediate capture reaches the objective, and one forcing-reply probe finds no equal recovery.

CLOSE-ONE-PLY-TACTIC
Close a reply when a named one-ply check, capture, fork, skewer, pin conversion, or mate threat reaches the objective, and one forcing-reply probe finds no equal recovery.

CLOSE-WIN-WITH-CHECK
Close a reply when the witness captures the target with check and every legal answer must answer that check without capturing the winning piece or restoring the material.

CLOSE-RECAPTURE
Close a reply when it captures our winning piece but an immediate named recapture restores the objective.

CLOSE-GAIN-CLAMP
After the material objective has been reached, close a reply unless it gives check, threatens mate in one, captures the piece that produced the gain, wins equal-or-greater material, or creates an immediate promotion threat.

CAPTURE-ORDER
When two captures are available against related targets, enter COMPARE. Try each through the opponent's strongest LIVE reply. Reject a capture order after its first refutation and fully verify the first surviving order.

HORIZON
Once check_horizon is reached, do not begin a new checking or positional theme. Permit only a required check answer, mate, CASH, or COMPARE. If none succeeds, terminate HORIZON rather than guess.

URGENCY
When behind material, a permitted own move must check, threaten mate, win material, or directly attack a named target. Otherwise terminate GIVEUP.

[proof boundary]

A material result is proved when every legal opponent reply has been TRYed or closed and the gain survives CLOSE-GAIN-CLAMP. Quiet positional compensation outside the clamp is intentionally ignored.
[predicate vocabulary]

Forcing predicates
check(move)
mate_in_1(move)
mate_threat(move)
legal_reply_classes=n
countercheck(move)
required_check_answer

Material predicates
attacked(piece,attacker)
loose(piece)
defenders(piece,[pieces])
sole_defender(defender,piece)
shared_defender(defender,[pieces])
overloaded(piece,[duties])
material_gain(value_or_target)

Geometry predicates
alignment(piece1,piece2,piece3...)
pin(attacker,pinned,king_or_queen)
skewer(attacker,front_target,rear_target)
fork(attacker,[targets])
xray(attacker,blocker,target)
discovered_attack(attacker,blocker,target)
clearance(piece,line_or_square)
interference(move,defensive_line)
deflection(move,defender,target)
line_blocker(piece,line)
open_file(file,pieces)

King and mobility predicates
restricted_mobility(king)
flight_squares(king,[squares])
back_rank_clamp(king)
mating_net([attackers],king)

Pawn predicates
passed_pawn(pawn)
promotion_threat(pawn)
promotion_square(square)
blockader(piece,pawn)

Conversion predicates
recapture(move)
capture_order([moves])
safe_retreat(piece,square)
gain_survives_forcing_probe

USES is an explicit list of these predicates. It is never an engine score. A candidate that cannot name what it USES is not generated.

[supplemental conversion rules]

CASH-SAFETY
Once the objective has been concretely reached, CASH may include one move that directly saves the piece producing the gain, completes a forced exchange, answers an immediate attack on the gain, or places a threatened queen safely. This is conversion, not a new quiet idea, and does not spend quiet_budget.

CLOSE-PROMOTION
Close a reply class when a named passed-pawn sequence promotes or forces a rook or minor piece to sacrifice itself, and the one forcing-reply probe finds no countercheck, mate, or equal promotion.

CLOSE-FORCED-TRADE
Close a reply when an immediate named exchange sequence reaches the declared material objective and no forcing intermezzo changes the balance.

[corpus conventions]

Each TRY consumes one position from try_budget. No trace below uses more than ten TRY positions.
Every opponent node names its LIVE reply or reply classes and closes all remaining legal replies with a policy rule.
The final PGN is a compact canonical solution line. Variations are included only when they carry an important reply-class witness.
The plain-English section restates the dominant predicate transitions in the training style: OBSERVE, THREAT, REPLIES, COMPARE, CASH, PROVED.

EXERCISE 51: Bishop capture to rook pin of the queen
======================================================================

CTT/1
id: woodpecker-51-pin-queen-to-king-after-bishop-capture
policy: forcing-clamp-1
source: https://lichess.org/study/4gzGL3Gt/JujNp3vR
fen: 5rk1/ppp2ppp/6q1/2b1P3/3r4/2N1BQ1b/PP3PPP/R3R1K1 b - - 0 1
objective: win the white queen for a rook and bishop
status: solved
main: 1...Bxg2
result: *
positions_entered: 7

[trace]

THINK OBSERVE.
SAW attacked(P@g2,B@h3); alignment(R@d4,g4,Q@f3,K@g1); Q@f3 becomes exposed when B@h3 moves.
CANDIDATES 1...Bxg2: captures(P@g2), attacks(Q@f3), prepares(Rg4), USES=[attacked,alignment].
TRY 1...Bxg2 BECAUSE PICK-OUR-MOVE: the capture attacks the queen and activates the rook pin.
THINK REPLIES(goal=win(Q@f3)).
REPLY 2.Qg3: saves the queen from Bxf3 but places it on the g-file with K@g1 :: LIVE.
REPLY 2.Qxg2: captures the bishop but permits 2...Rg4, pinning Q@g2 to K@g1 :: CLOSED BY CLOSE-ONE-PLY-TACTIC WITH 2...Rg4.
REPLY CLASS all other legal replies: either leave Q@f3 capturable by Bxf3 or permit the same rook pin without improving on Qg3 :: CLOSED BY CLOSE-ABANDON WITH Bxf3 or Rg4.
TRY 2.Qg3 BECAUSE TRY-REPLIES: it is the reply that preserves the queen longest.
THINK OBSERVE.
SAW alignment(R@d4,Q@g3,K@g1); Q@g3 has no safe capture of R@g4 because Q@g6 recaptures.
CANDIDATES 2...Rg4: attacks(Q@g3), pins(Q@g3,K@g1), USES=[alignment].
TRY 2...Rg4 BECAUSE PICK-OUR-MOVE: it converts the observed alignment into a pin.
THINK REPLIES(goal=win(Q@g3)).
REPLY 3.Bxc5: captures(B@c5) before the pinned queen is lost :: LIVE.
REPLY 3.Qxg4: captures the rook but permits Qxg4, preserving the queen gain :: CLOSED BY CLOSE-RECAPTURE WITH 3...Qxg4.
REPLY CLASS all other legal replies: do not remove the pin or give an equal forcing recovery :: CLOSED BY CLOSE-ABANDON WITH Rxg3.
TRY 3.Bxc5 BECAUSE TRY-REPLIES: it is White's strongest intermezzo.
THINK CASH(target=Q@g3).
TRY 3...Rxg3 BECAUSE the pinned queen is now capturable.
THINK REPLIES(goal=queen_for_rook_and_bishop).
REPLY 4.fxg3: recaptures the rook; Black has still exchanged rook and bishop for queen and pawn :: LIVE.
REPLY 4.hxg3: has the same material result :: CLOSED BY CLOSE-RECAPTURE WITH the recorded queen-for-rook-and-bishop balance.
REPLY CLASS all other legal replies: leave the queen gone without recovering the rook :: CLOSED BY CLOSE-GAIN-CLAMP.
TRY 4.fxg3 BECAUSE TRY-REPLIES: capture the winning piece first.
THINK CASH(target=material_gain).
TRY 4...Re8 BECAUSE CASH-SAFETY: activate the remaining rook while the material gain is secure.
PROVED.

[plain english]

OBSERVE
  the bishop can take g2 and attack the queen on f3
  the rook on d4 can swing to g4
  a queen placed on the g-file will stand in front of the king on g1

THREAT
  Bxg2 attacks the queen and prepares the rook pin

REPLIES
  Qxg2 loses the queen to Rg4 because the queen is pinned to the king
  Qg3 is the best attempt to keep the queen
  other replies either leave the queen to Bxf3 or permit the same pin

OBSERVE
  after Qg3 the rook, queen, and king are aligned on the g-file

THREAT
  Rg4 pins the queen to the king

REPLIES
  Bxc5 is White's useful intermezzo before the queen is lost
  no reply actually breaks the pin

CASH
  Rxg3 wins the queen
  fxg3 recaptures the rook, but Black has traded a rook and bishop for the queen
  Re8 consolidates the gain

PROVED

======================================================================

CTT/1
id: woodpecker-52-queen-decoy-to-pawn-mate
policy: forcing-clamp-1
source: https://lichess.org/study/4gzGL3Gt/0xdiaEHQ
fen: 4r2k/ppp3pp/8/1PPb1p2/3P1P1b/P1Q2p1P/7R/R4KBq b - - 0 1
objective: force checkmate
status: solved
main: 1...Qg2+
result: *
positions_entered: 3

[trace]

THINK OBSERVE.
SAW restricted_mobility(K@f1); P@f3 controls(g2); R@h2 is the only piece able to capture a queen on g2.
CANDIDATES 1...Qg2+: gives_check, legal_reply_classes=1, decoys(R@h2,g2), USES=[restricted_mobility,decoy].
TRY 1...Qg2+ BECAUSE PICK-OUR-MOVE: it is a check with one legal reply.
THINK REPLIES(goal=mate).
REPLY 2.Rxg2: only_legal_reply; places R@g2 on the pawn's capture square :: LIVE.
TRY 2.Rxg2 BECAUSE ALL-REPLIES.
THINK CASH(target=K@f1).
SAW mate_in_1(2...fxg2#).
CANDIDATES 2...fxg2#: mate, USES=[pawn_control,restricted_mobility].
TRY 2...fxg2# BECAUSE PICK-OUR-MOVE: mate.
PROVED.

EXERCISE 53: Pawn check to queen sacrifice and back-rank mate
======================================================================

CTT/1
id: woodpecker-53-pawn-check-to-queen-sacrifice-back-rank-mate
policy: forcing-clamp-1
source: https://lichess.org/study/4gzGL3Gt/xNFQIvEs
fen: 3r1b1k/pp4p1/2p1Qp2/5N2/PP2Pp2/2Pq4/5PKP/5R2 b - - 0 1
objective: force checkmate or win the rook on f1
status: solved
main: 1...f3+
result: *
positions_entered: 5

[trace]

THINK OBSERVE.
SAW restricted_mobility(K@g2); alignment(Q@d3,R@f1); R@d8 can enter d1 after K reaches f1.
CANDIDATES 1...f3+: gives_check, drives(K@g2), preserves(Qxf1+), USES=[restricted_mobility,alignment].
TRY 1...f3+ BECAUSE PICK-OUR-MOVE: the check moves the king while keeping the rook capture.
THINK REPLIES(goal=mate_or_win(R@f1)).
REPLY 2.Kg1: permits(Qxf1+) with a forced king recapture :: LIVE.
REPLY 2.Kh1: permits(Qxf1#) :: CLOSED BY CLOSE-MATE-IN-ONE WITH 2...Qxf1#.
REPLY CLASS [2.Kg3,2.Kh3]: leave R@f1 capturable by Qxf1, meeting the material objective :: CLOSED BY CLOSE-ONE-PLY-TACTIC WITH 2...Qxf1.
TRY 2.Kg1 BECAUSE TRY-REPLIES: it is the defense that forces Black to prove the full mating line.
THINK OBSERVE.
SAW Q@d3 can capture R@f1 with check; K@g1 is the only recapturer.
CANDIDATES 2...Qxf1+: captures(R@f1), gives_check, legal_reply_classes=1, USES=[alignment].
TRY 2...Qxf1+ BECAUSE PICK-OUR-MOVE: checking capture with one reply.
THINK REPLIES(goal=mate).
REPLY 3.Kxf1: only_legal_reply; places K@f1 on the d1 rook line :: LIVE.
TRY 3.Kxf1 BECAUSE ALL-REPLIES.
THINK CASH(target=K@f1).
SAW mate_in_1(3...Rd1#).
TRY 3...Rd1# BECAUSE PICK-OUR-MOVE: mate.
PROVED.

EXERCISE 54: Bishop decoy to queen check and loose rook
======================================================================

CTT/1
id: woodpecker-54-bishop-decoy-to-queen-check-and-loose-rook
policy: forcing-clamp-1
source: https://lichess.org/study/4gzGL3Gt/RqwKWkd3
fen: r1b2rk1/pp4pp/2pb4/3p1pq1/2PP4/1N1BPR2/PPQ3PP/4R1K1 b - - 0 1
objective: win the rook on e1 by force
status: solved
main: 1...Bxh2+
result: *
positions_entered: 5

[trace]

THINK OBSERVE.
SAW loose(R@e1); Q@g5 has diagonal(h4,e1); K@g1 can be drawn toward h2; B@d6 can give Bxh2+.
CANDIDATES 1...Bxh2+: gives_check, decoys(K@g1,h2), opens(Qh4+), USES=[loose,alignment,decoy].
TRY 1...Bxh2+ BECAUSE PICK-OUR-MOVE: the check draws the king onto the queen's checking route.
THINK REPLIES(goal=win(R@e1)).
REPLY 2.Kxh2: captures the bishop and permits Qh4+ :: LIVE.
REPLY CLASS [2.Kf1,2.Kh1]: decline the bishop but permit Qxg2+ while R@e1 remains loose :: CLOSED BY CLOSE-ONE-PLY-TACTIC WITH 2...Qxg2+.
REPLY 2.Kf2: permits Qh4+ or Qxe3+, preserving the attack and material gain :: CLOSED BY CLOSE-ONE-PLY-TACTIC WITH 2...Qh4+.
TRY 2.Kxh2 BECAUSE TRY-REPLIES: capture the attacker.
THINK OBSERVE.
SAW Q@g5 can check from h4 and remains aligned with R@e1.
CANDIDATES 2...Qh4+: gives_check, attacks(R@e1), legal_reply_classes=2, USES=[alignment,loose].
TRY 2...Qh4+ BECAUSE PICK-OUR-MOVE: the check keeps the rook capture attached.
THINK REPLIES(goal=win(R@e1)).
REPLY 3.Rh3: blocks the check but does not defend R@e1 :: LIVE.
REPLY 3.Kg1: leaves R@e1 immediately capturable :: CLOSED BY CLOSE-ONE-PLY-TACTIC WITH 3...Qxe1.
TRY 3.Rh3 BECAUSE TRY-REPLIES: block while preserving the most material.
THINK CASH(target=R@e1).
TRY 3...Qxe1 BECAUSE the rook is loose and the check has ended.
REPLY CLASS all legal replies: no forcing recovery of equal value exists within the clamp :: CLOSED BY CLOSE-GAIN-CLAMP.
PROVED.

EXERCISE 55: Exchange sacrifice clears the queen line
======================================================================

CTT/1
id: woodpecker-55-exchange-sacrifice-clears-queen-line
policy: forcing-clamp-1
source: https://lichess.org/study/4gzGL3Gt/9dpHUtaw
fen: q6r/1b4bp/4k1p1/1p2Pn2/2pPp1Q1/2P5/1P1N2PP/2B2RK1 w - - 0 1
objective: capture Nf5 and Bg7 while opening the black king
status: solved
main: 1.Rxf5
result: *
positions_entered: 3

[trace]

THINK OBSERVE.
SAW attacked(N@f5,R@f1); alignment(Q@g4,P@g6,B@g7); P@g6 blocks Q@g4 from B@g7.
CANDIDATES 1.Rxf5: captures(N@f5), invites(gxf5), clears(Q@g4,B@g7), USES=[attacked,alignment,clearance].
TRY 1.Rxf5 BECAUSE PICK-OUR-MOVE: the capture removes a knight and offers the rook to clear the queen's line.
THINK REPLIES(goal=capture(N@f5,B@g7)).
REPLY 1...gxf5: captures the rook and vacates g6 :: LIVE.
REPLY 1...Bxe5: creates a countercapture but leaves R@f5 after it has won N@f5 :: CLOSED BY CLOSE-GAIN-CLAMP.
REPLY CLASS all other legal replies: do not recover the captured knight and do not create an equal forcing threat :: CLOSED BY CLOSE-GAIN-CLAMP.
TRY 1...gxf5 BECAUSE TRY-REPLIES: capture the winning rook.
THINK CASH(target=B@g7).
SAW loose(B@g7); Q@g4 now attacks g7.
CANDIDATES 2.Qxg7: captures(B@g7), checks the material objective, USES=[loose,clearance].
TRY 2.Qxg7 BECAUSE the blocker has been deflected from g6.
REPLY CLASS all legal replies: the declared capture objective is complete; only forcing king counterplay is retained, and none restores both minor pieces :: CLOSED BY CLOSE-GAIN-CLAMP.
PROVED.
